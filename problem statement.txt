GeoAI Hackathon @ CSRE, IIT Bombay
Build an AI assistant (SLM) that recommends where and when to head outdoors
The challenge
Weather forecasts and maps tell you what the conditions will be. What people really want to
know is more specific: which nearby place, at what time, is best for me, and what should I keep
in mind before I go?
Build an AI assistant (Small Language Model), powered by a small language model, that
advises a person on an outdoor activity: the best nearby place and time to go, what to prepare,
what to watch for, and when to avoid it. The assistant should reason over geospatial context
and live weather to arrive at its recommendation.
Given a free-form question, the assistant should:
● identify the activity, the area or search radius, the time range, and any personal detail
such as age or a health condition;
● consider several nearby places across the upcoming hours, rather than a single
location;
● account for geospatial context, including wind direction, roads and traffic, nearby
industry, water bodies and green cover;
● return a clear recommendation together with practical advice: what to carry, what to
watch for, and when to avoid going;
● support every claim with the live data it actually used, without inventing weather or
places;
● present the response in whatever form works best, whether plain text or something
richer with maps and images.
What makes the problem interesting: the right answer depends on both the hour and the
place. A ground that lies downwind of an expressway at 8 AM may be pleasant by evening
once the wind shifts, and a venue forty minutes away at rush hour may not be worth the trip.
The assistant has to reason about place and time together, for this particular person.
At a glance
Nothing is supplied in advance. For each question, the assistant fetches what it needs in real
time, reasons about place and time using a small language model, and returns a grounded
recommendation with supporting advice.
Activities to support
Treat the following as your reference set. Your assistant should support at least these ten
outdoor activities:
● Running or jogging
● Cycling (leisure or commute)
● Golf
● Cricket (recreational match or practice)
● Outdoor football or five-a-side
● Outdoor tennis
● Hiking or a nature walk
● Picnic or park outing
● Outdoor event or gathering (for example a wedding or fair)
● Outdoor photography session
Each activity has its own sensitivities to weather, air quality and daylight, so the same
conditions can suit one and rule out another. Personal profiles, such as asthma, a senior, a
child or pregnancy, should tighten the limits further for that user.
Example Queries
The following are the kinds of free-form questions your assistant should handle. They illustrate
the input only; how you shape the response is up to you.
● "Where can I go running tomorrow evening near Koregaon Park?"
● "Best time this weekend for a kids' cricket match within 15 km of Whitefield?"
● "I have mild asthma, where's good for a morning walk near Powai in the next two
days?"
● "Is it okay to cycle to work at 8 AM?"
● "Planning an outdoor birthday party on Sunday afternoon in Pune, what should I keep in
mind?"
● "Good place and time for golf on Saturday within 40 km of Andheri?"
● "Where can I take sunset photos this evening around Bandra?"
● "Is Saturday or Sunday better for a hike near Lonavala?"
● "When is it safe for my parents to walk outdoors tomorrow in Bengaluru?"
● "Outdoor tennis after work today near Hinjewadi, any good spots?"
Expect incomplete or messy questions as well: a missing location, a vague time such as "this
weekend", or a request that falls outside the scope. Handle these gracefully rather than
guessing. This is just for your understanding the evaluation queries would be different
then these.
Data: fetch it live
No dataset is provided. We supply no map files, no weather files and no server. Your
assistant must fetch everything it needs live, at query time, from free and open sources.
Sourcing and handling that data is part of the challenge, since a real assistant has to do the
same.
The sources below are a starting point, not a requirement. You may use any free source,
provided you disclose it. Respect each service's rate limits, and cache responses sensibly.
Maps, places and features (venues, roads, industrial zones, water, parks, power lines)
● OpenStreetMap via the Overpass API.
● Geofabrik: downloadable regional OSM extracts
● Nominatim: geocoding (place name - coordinates)
Weather and air quality
● Open-Meteo: forecast + air-quality APIs
● OpenAQ: open air-quality measurements
● WAQI: real-time AQI, free token
● OpenWeatherMap: free tier, needs a key (alternative)
Routing and Travel Time
● OSRM or OpenRouteservice
Daylight and terrain
● Sunrise-sunset API, Open-Elevation/ OpenTopoData
Indian Open data
● Data.gov.in and CPCP for air quality and IMD for weather
You are free to use any other free and open source beyond those listed. Whatever you use,
you must explicitly state your data sources.
Your model: a small open SLM on Hugging Face
The language component of your assistant must be a small, open-weights model of at most
3 billion parameters that you run yourself. A small model cannot memorise the world's
weather and geography, and that limitation is the point: it forces the system to rely on live data
rather than recall.
You are not expected to train a model from scratch; pretraining is beyond the scope of a
hackathon. You may use an open base model directly (for example Phi-3-mini, Llama-3.2,
Qwen2.5 or Gemma-2), or fine-tune one (for example with LoRA or QLoRA). Either approach
is acceptable, and the choice is yours.
Host your model on the Hugging Face Hub as a public repository. Include the weights (or
your LoRA adapter together with a pointer to the exact base model), a model card describing
the base model, your changes and intended use, and an open licence. It should
load with from_pretrained(...). If you use a base model unchanged, still create a repository with
your configuration and a model card naming the base model and how you use it.
Large hosted models, such as GPT-4-class systems, Claude or Gemini Pro, may not be used
within the running assistant.
Deliverables
Each team submits the following:

2. Your model on Hugging Face — a public repository with the model or adapter, a model

Rules
Allowed
● Any open-weights language model of at most 3B parameters (disclose the exact
model).
● Fine-tuning that model (LoRA / QLoRA or similar).
● Any free, openly available data source or API — each disclosed.
● Any open-source libraries, and AI coding assistants for writing code (disclosed).
Not allowed
● Large hosted models (GPT-4-class, Claude, Gemini Pro) anywhere in the running
assistant.
● Paid or private data or APIs; scraping behind logins.
● Hard-coding answers to the example questions.
● Answering from the model's memory instead of the data it fetched.
Disclose the models, data sources and tools you used, and be prepared to explain and defend
your work during judging.

FAQ
Do we have to train a model from scratch? No. Use an open model of at most 3 billion
parameters. Fine-tuning is optional, and pretraining is not expected.
Can we use ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini? Not within the running assistant. You may use
them as coding aids while you build, provided you disclose it.
Where does the data come from? You fetch it live from free, open APIs, listed under Data.
No dataset is provided.
Do we need a GPU? Not to run the assistant. If you choose to fine-tune, a free Colab or
Kaggle GPU is sufficient.
What if our API endpoint is unavailable during judging? Keep it live for the full judging
window. If we cannot reach it, we will rely on your code, README and demo, which is less
ideal, so test your hosting early.
Can we support more than ten activities? Yes. Ten is the minimum; additional,
well-handled activities are welcome.
What is the team size? To be announced.
